	\begin{block}{Introduction} \label{intro}
		Ice cover in the Great Lakes  highly variable and has significant influence on regional climate, ecology, and economy. Spatially resolved ice cover ($\Delta x=1.25 \textup{KM}^2$) has been recorded since 1973 and is available as daily files throughout each ice season\supercite{Yang2020}. Previous work has assessed large-scale (i.e. lake or basin-wide) ice cover fluctuations through time and their relationship to global weather phenomena \supercite{Mason2016, Sharma2019, OReilly2015, Wang2018, Bai2012}. Following Mason et al. (2016)\supercite{Mason2016} we use the Great Lakes Aquatic Habitat Framework (GLAHF)\supercite{Wang2015} to analyze ice cover and local meteorlogical data for 1973-present and ask two research questions:

	  \normalsize \bfseries
	  \begin{enumerate}
		  \item{Do significant ice trends exist at these spatial scales?}
		  \item{Are correlations between atmosphere and ice data consistent with our understanding of physical processes?}
 	 \end{enumerate}

\begin{column}{\colwidth}
	\justifying
	To address the first research question, the below metrics are calculated for each GLAHF subregion and ice season and then Mann-Kendall monotonic trend test\supercite{Mann1945} is applied to check for statistical significance:
	\begin{itemize}
		  \item \textbf{Annual Max Ice Cover (AMIC):} maximum percent during a given season
		  \item \textbf{Seasonal Average Ice Cover (JFM):} mean daily ice cover:  Jan 1 - Mar 31
		  \item \textbf{Ice Duration:} Total (non-consecutive) days$^{\dagger}$ where ice cover exceeded 10\%
		\end{itemize}



	To address the second research question, station data\supercite{Menne2012,Smith2011} is aggregated for each subregion based on a 20 KM radius and correlations between ice metrics and daily mean meteorological surface variables are identified. On average, 30 stations per subregion were located within the radius but station data availability varied through time, location and variable. Temperature min/max were reported most frequently (median station count $\approx$10 per subregion). Other variables\textemdash cloud cover, precipitation, wind speed, were reported less frequently (median $\approx$5).


	\vspace{1ex}
	\scriptsize{	$\dagger$: days are only counted Jan 1 - Mar 31 due to shorter ice reporting windows during 1970's and 80's. This method likely \emph{underestimates} trends in decreasing ice duration but is required to avoid artificial season length decrease.}
	\vspace{2ex}

			\justifying
			Season duration decrease is visible in some regions but not apparent in others (see fig. \ref{fig:dur}).  Regions that show decrease are often offshore and deep: Superior (WSU, CSU, ESU), Huron (NHU, EHU, CHU), and Ontario (WON, CON). This may be due to warmer subsurface temps\supercite{Cannon2024, Anderson2021} during later years providing heat to surface waters and preventing freezing, whereas shallow regions may be able to consistently release all heat from the water column.

		\justifying


	As expected, ice cover showed strong inverse correlation with air temperature. Wind speed showed moderate correlation to ice in some regions which may be due to wind's influence on heat distribution in the water column via mixing (though this impact is more relevant during fall overturn). The analyses presented here are a small fraction of possible correlation pairs and more work is needed, including different time periods and other metrics like ice onset/offset.

\begin{block}{Summary}\label{summary}
		\underline{Trend assessment:}
		\vspace{-2ex}
		\begin{itemize}
			\item A majority of subregions show significant decreasing trends in all three metrics
			\item Seasonal average trends were significant in \underline{ALL} 22 subregions
			\item Max Ice Cover showed the least significant trends (17/22 regions)
		\end{itemize}
	\vspace{-2ex}
		\underline{Met Data Correlation:}
		\vspace{-2ex}
		\begin{itemize}
			\item Air temperature was strongly anti-correlated with ice cover for all metrics
			\item More work needed: comprehensive assessment of ice cover and met data correlations
		\end{itemize}
  \end{block}
